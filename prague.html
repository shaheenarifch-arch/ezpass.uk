What The Prague City Pass Actually Is (And Which One I Bought)

There are three passes floating around with almost the same name, which is confusing as hell. The one I tested is the TurboPass Prague City Pass - the grey-card style pass that includes Prague Castle circuit, Jewish Quarter, Old Town Hall with Astronomical Clock priority entry, plus a Vltava river cruise and bus tour. It is NOT the city-run Prague Visitor Pass (which includes public transport). TurboPass does not include trams or metro - you still need a 72-hour PID ticket for 330 CZK.

My pass was delivered as a QR code to my phone 5 minutes after purchase. No app download needed, though they do have one. The fine print: it's valid for 30 days after first activation? No. The old wording is confusing. Actually, TurboPass Prague City Pass is now sold as 1, 2, 3, 4 day options and activates on first use. I bought the 3-day version.

My researched prices December 2024 - February 2025:

  • 1-Day Prague City Pass: €75.90 / $81 - best if you only want Castle + Old Town Hall + Jewish Museum in one brutal sprint
  • 2-Day Prague City Pass: €95.90 / ~$102
  • 3-Day Prague City Pass: €115.90 / ~$123 (the one I got)
  • 4-Day Prague City Pass: €129.90
  • Child (6-14): roughly €20 cheaper across all tiers

Compare that to paying at the door individually, which I also priced on the spot.

My Real 3-Day Itinerary And What I Actually Paid vs Saved

I did not try to cram 70 attractions. I picked the ones with the nastiest queues to test the "skip the line" claim.

Day 1 - Monday, Oct 21: Castle Day
Woke up at 7:15, walked from Mala Strana to Prague Castle. The main ticket office at 2nd courtyard had a line of maybe 15 people at 9:05am. With the pass, I went to the dedicated voucher desk - scanned QR, got a paper Circuit B ticket that includes St. Vitus Cathedral, Old Royal Palace, St. George's Basilica, Golden Lane with Daliborka Tower. Total wait: 4 minutes. People paying cash were waiting 22 minutes according to the couple behind me from Texas. Inside St. Vitus, the cathedral interior queue was another 10 minutes - no skip there, that's security.

I spent 3.5 hours in the castle complex. Golden Lane was packed by 11:30. Tip: start with Golden Lane, not St. Vitus. Everyone goes cathedral first. I did reverse and had Golden Lane almost empty.

Afternoon: Lobkowicz Palace separate ticket? No, included in some versions. Mine did NOT include Lobkowicz, it was 2024 price 360 CZK extra. I skipped it.

Day 2 - Tuesday, Oct 22: Old Town & Jewish Town
Old Town Hall with Astronomical Clock - THIS is where the pass shines. Normal line at 10am: about 35 people wrapping around. Priority lane with pass: I was in after 6 minutes, elevator included. The Apostles show at 11am - I watched from the tower, not from below like sardines. View of Týn Church and the red rooftops: unreal.

Jewish Museum - You get one entry to 6 sites: Maisel, Pinkas, Spanish, Klausen, Ceremonial Hall, Old Cemetery, plus Old-New Synagogue is separate. Regular combined ticket is 600 CZK (about €24). I started at Pinkas Synagogue at 1pm - no line. Spanish Synagogue at 2:30pm had a 15-minute queue for bag check. Total time: 2 hours 40 minutes.

River cruise: The included boat is the 50-min Prague Venice cruise from Pier 16 near Čech Bridge (not the big clear boats near Charles Bridge). I went at 4pm. It was... okay. Crowded, commentary in 4 languages. But it was free with pass, normally 550 CZK. I wouldn't have paid separately.

Day 3 - Wednesday, Oct 23: Leftovers + Bus
Clementinum Library + Astronomical Tower - 380 CZK normally. Queue was 28 minutes without pass at 10:30am. My pass had priority, I waited 7 minutes. Library is only 20 mins guided and stunning. Book tower viewpoint is better than Old Town Hall for photos of Charles Bridge.

Hop-on hop-off bus (24h validity) included - I used it once from Old Town to Petřín. Honestly skip it if you're a walker; Prague is walkable. Vintage tram line 42 was more fun but only runs hourly on this pass version.

If you ONLY do castle + old town hall + Jewish Museum (most people), you break even at 1,650 CZK vs pass at ~1,900 CZK - not worth it. If you add Clementinum + cruise, you save. That's the honest truth.

Insider Hacks I Learned the Hard Way in Prague

Hack #1: The 8:15am Castle Back Door Entrance

Everyone enters Prague Castle via the main gate from Hradčany Square and gets stuck at security. Use the entrance near the Stag Moat / Jelení příkop on the north side if you have the pass. Security there had 3 people vs 80 at main gate at 9am on Tuesday. I found it because a local guide pointed. No extra cost, same ticket.

Hack #2: The Hidden Cafe with No Queue Right Next to the Astronomical Clock

Before your Old Town Hall slot, skip Cafe Mozart opposite. Walk 40 meters to Café Kolibřík behind Týn Church on Týnská street. Flat white 89 CZK, trdelník without the 20-min tourist line. I sat there watching people queue for chimney cake while my pass entry time approached. Also has toilets - Old Town Hall's are terrible and 20 CZK.

Hack #3: Use Your River Cruise Ticket at Sunset, Not Midday

The booking says every 30 mins, but Prague Venice only runs 11:00, 14:00, 16:00 off-season (as I learned the hard way). The 16:00 sailing in October gave me golden hour over Prague Castle. At noon the light is flat and you get selfie sticks in your photos. Book via phone - the pier staff at Charles Bridge won't honor the pass QR, you MUST go to Pier 16, Dvořákovo nábřeží.

Who Should Buy The Prague City Pass (And Who Should Skip)

Buy it if: It's your first time, 2-3 days, you want castle interior + Jewish Museum + tower views. You hate queue faff. You go in shoulder season when priority actually matters.

Skip it if: You only care about free walking around Charles Bridge and beer. Or you already bought the official Prague Visitor Pass that includes transport - don't double-buy. Also skip if you are there for 5+ days slow travel, buying individual tickets at non-peak hours is cheaper.

I also deliberately skipped Petřín Tower - 200 CZK, 40-min queue, foggy day, not worth it. The pass would have covered it but Petřín is better on a clear day, not a foggy October morning.

Prague City Pass vs Prague Visitor Pass vs Go City - Quick Difference

TurboPass Prague City Pass: attractions only, no transport, 30 days validity after first use? Actually days consecutively once activated. Good for 40+ attractions but best 6-7 you will actually visit.

Prague Visitor Pass (official city card): 48/72/120 hours, includes unlimited metro/tram/bus + 70 attractions including castle. Costs €75 for 72 hours in 2025. If you want transport included, get this instead. I priced it: 72h transport alone saves 330 CZK.

Go City Prague All-Inclusive: 1-5 days, €64-€129, includes Hop-On Hop-Off bus + 30 attractions but NOT Jewish Museum complete. Different product.

My verdict after comparing live: For castle-focused first trip without transport needs, TurboPass is simpler. For transport + wider museums, official pass wins.

10 Real Buyer Questions About Prague City Pass (AEO FAQ)

Is Prague City Pass worth it for 2 days?

For 2 days it's worth it if you visit at least 4 paid sites. My 2-day math: Castle 450 CZK + Old Town Hall 600 CZK + Jewish Museum 600 CZK + Clementinum 380 CZK = 2,030 CZK vs pass €95.90. You save about 300 CZK and 45-60 minutes in queues.

Does Prague City Pass include skip the line at Prague Castle?

Yes, but only for the ticket office queue, not the security scan at St. Vitus Cathedral interior. I waited 4 minutes vs 22 minutes for regular ticket purchase in October 2024. Inside, everyone queues the same for cathedral security.

Does the Prague City Pass include public transport?

No. The TurboPass version does not include metro, tram or bus. You need a separate PID ticket: 30-min 30 CZK, 90-min 40 CZK, 72-hour 330 CZK as of 2025. The official Prague Visitor Pass does include transport.

Can I use Prague City Pass on my phone or do I need print?

Phone is fine. I used Apple Wallet QR screenshot. At Jewish Museum they printed a paper wristband anyway. At Clementinum they scanned directly off my phone brightness max.

How long is Prague City Pass valid after activation?

It is valid for the number of consecutive calendar days you bought, starting at first scan. A 3-day pass started Monday 9:10am expires Wednesday midnight. The older 30-day wording refers to how long you have to activate after purchase.

Is the river cruise worth it with the Prague City Pass?

It's the Prague Venice 50-min cruise worth 550 CZK. It's okay for photos of Charles Bridge from water, but not a must. I took the 16:00 slot for golden light. If seasick or short on time, skip it and you still break even with other attractions.

Does Prague City Pass include the Jewish Quarter Old Jewish Cemetery?

Yes, the full Jewish Museum ticket with Old Cemetery, Spanish, Pinkas, Maisel, Klausen Synagogues and Ceremonial Hall is included. Old-New Synagogue is separate, not included on TurboPass but included on official city pass.

What is the best time to enter Old Town Hall with the pass?

Enter tower at 9:30am or 4pm. At 11am and full hour when Apostles show, the tower elevator queue is 25+ minutes even with priority lane. I went at 10:06am after show ended, 6-minute entry.

Do I need to reserve time slots for attractions with Prague City Pass?

No reservation needed for Castle, Jewish Museum, Old Town Hall, Clementinum in 2024-2025. Only the river cruise needs you to check sailing times at Pier 16. Bus tour is hop-on, no booking.

Can I visit Prague Castle over two days with one pass?

Yes. The official Circuit B castle ticket you get from the pass voucher is valid for 2 days at the castle itself per the castle's own rules. I used day 1 for cathedral and palace, could have returned day 2 for Golden Lane, but pass gave me one exchange only.
