What the Berlin City Pass Actually Is (And What It Isn't)

The product sold as Berlin City Pass is officially the Berlin WelcomeCard All-Inclusive powered by TurboPass. It is NOT the normal Berlin WelcomeCard (which only gives discounts). This one gives free entry to 30+ attractions + unlimited U-Bahn, S-Bahn, bus, tram in zones ABC + hop-on hop-off + Spree boat in summer.

When I bought it in February 2025 for my March trip, these were the official Turbopass prices I paid/screen-shotted:

  • 2 Days (48h): €124 adult without transport / €144 with ABC transport – child (6-14) €74 / €84
  • 3 Days (72h): €159 / €179 with transport – child €94 / €104
  • 6 Days: €199 / €229 with transport – child €119 / €139

Prices moved up since 2023 – the old €79 base is gone. I saw it fluctuate €5-10 depending on season, but March pricing above is accurate. The transport add-on is absolutely worth it in Berlin because a single AB ticket is now €3.50 and you'd need 3-4 a day.

My 3-Day Tested Route: What I Chose and What I Skipped

I based myself near Alexanderplatz (Mitte) because almost everything on the pass is walkable from there or one S-Bahn stop. I wanted to test if you can actually do Museum Island without going insane.

Day 1 – Tuesday: Museum Island Blitz
I started at 9:15am at Neues Museum – there were 6 people ahead. With the pass I walked straight to the group gate, scanned QR, in by 9:22. Without it, regular queue was already 25 minutes at 9:40. Spent 1h40 there. Then 11:10 Pergamon Panorama (the temporary Pergamon while the main Pergamon Museum is closed for renovation till 2027) – pass included, saved €19. Bode Museum at 13:30 – empty, no queue, 45 mins. Altes Museum at 15:00 – in instantly. By 16:10 I was at Berlin TV Tower – this is where the pass shines: normal ticket sellers said 90-minute wait for walk-ups, my reserved slot via TurboPass app let me up in 12 minutes. Sunset from the tower at 17:45 was worth it alone.

Day 2 – Wednesday: Wall, Spies, and a Mistake
8:30am DDR Museum – I thought it opened at 9, but it opened at 9, so I waited with coffee. At 9:05 I was second in line, in by 9:09. This museum is tiny and gets jammed after 11am – go early. 10:30 German Spy Museum – pass covered €16, no queue at all. 12:30 Hop-on Hop-off bus (included) – I used it as transport to Charlottenburg, not as a tour. 14:00 Charlottenburg Palace – pass includes it, but it's far west. Honestly, I'd skip this if you're short on time; it was pretty but 35 mins each way on bus. 16:30 The Wall Museum at East Side Gallery – €13.50 saved, walked in.

Day 3 – Thursday: Potsdam and the Boat
I activated the ABC transport option. Took S7 to Potsdam at 8:02, bus 695 to Sanssouci. The pass includes Sanssouci Palace entry – huge win, that's €22. I was there at 9:30, no queue. Back to Berlin by 13:00, did the Stern & Kreisschiffahrt Spree cruise (1h) – it's only included April-October, but they opened early due to warm week, I got on free (€28 saved). Finished at Jewish Museum Berlin – free with pass, €12 saved.

3 Insider Hacks I Learned The Hard Way

1. The Museum Island Back Entrance Trick

Everyone queues at the main Neues Museum door on Bodestrasse. With the City Pass, you can enter via the James-Simon-Galerie central entrance – the security there processes pass holders faster. I saved 18 minutes on Day 1 just by following the "Groups & City Pass" sign to the left, not the main line. Go before 10am. After 11am, Museum Island is school groups hell.

2. TV Tower Reservation – Do It Day -1

Your pass doesn't auto-reserve the TV Tower. In the TurboPass app, click TV Tower > Reserve slot, pick next day 16:30-17:30 for sunset. I almost missed this – if you just show up, they send you to the back office to book a slot 2 hours later. Book at your hotel the night before. The best light is last hour before sunset, and the pass queue is separate and moves 3x faster.

3. The Cafe No One Tells You About Near East Side Gallery

After The Wall Museum, skip the tourist currywurst stand. Walk 3 minutes north to Silo Coffee (Gabriel-Max-Str 2). Best flat white in Friedrichshain, and you can sit and actually see the Spree. I wasted €7.50 on a bad currywurst at the Gallery before I found it. Also, the pass's Spree boat leaves from near Friedrichstrasse – if you go in winter, take the 1h tour at 14:00 when sun hits the dome.

What I Loved vs What Annoyed Me

Loved: One QR code for everything, transport genuinely unlimited (I tapped U8 eight times a day), no printing, Potsdam included without extra ticket, fast-track at TV Tower and DDR Museum.

Annoyed: Pergamon Museum main building is still closed until 2027 – they replace it with Panorama, which is cool but not the same. Some attractions require separate reservation (TV Tower, Potsdam palace timed entry) – if you miss the fine print, you queue anyway. Hop-on bus audio was crackly in German/English overlap.

Berlin City Pass vs Berlin WelcomeCard vs Museum Pass – Quick Take

If you only want transport + discounts, get the regular WelcomeCard (€29.50 for 72h). If you only want museums, the Museum Pass Berlin (€32 for 3 days) covers 30 museums but no tower, no boat, no transport. The City Pass/All-Inclusive is the only one that bundles ABC transport + 30 attractions + bus + boat. For a first-time visitor doing 2+ museums a day, it's the best value. For a local or second-time visitor who just wants nightlife, it's overkill.

Who Should Actually Buy The Berlin City Pass?

Buy it if: first time, 2-6 days, you like to tick boxes, you stay in Mitte/Prenzlauer Berg, you hate buying single tickets, you go March-October when boat runs.
Skip it if: you have 1 day only (do single tickets), you only care about clubs/techno, you have kids under 6 (they get many museums free anyway), you stay in Neukölln and plan to just eat and park.

FAQs – Real Buyer Questions About Berlin City Pass

Is the Berlin City Pass worth it for 2 days?

Yes, if you do at least 3 paid sights per day. My 2-day math was TV Tower €26.50 + 2 Museum Island museums €44 + DDR Museum €13.50 + transport €22 = €106. Pass is €144 with transport, so you break even quickly. For 2 packed days, it saves €40-60.

Does Berlin City Pass include public transport from the airport?

Only if you buy the ABC version. The AB version stops at city border, BER airport is zone C. I bought ABC for €20 extra and rode RE7 from Flughafen BER to Alexanderplatz free. Get ABC if you fly in.

Do I still need to queue with the Berlin City Pass?

You skip ticket-buying queues, but not security lines. At Neues Museum I waited 7 minutes for security vs 25 minutes for ticket buyers. At TV Tower you get a separate fast-track lane with reserved time, saving 60-90 minutes in high season.

Is the Pergamon Museum included right now in 2025?

The main Pergamon building is closed for full renovation until at least 2027. The City Pass includes Pergamon Panorama exhibition on Museum Island instead. Same ticket price, different experience – 360-degree ancient city panorama, still worth it.

Can I use Berlin City Pass on my phone or do I need to print?

Phone only is fine. The TurboPass app gives a QR code that works offline after activation. I never printed. Keep brightness up and have ID – TV Tower asked for name match.

How does the hop-on hop-off work with the pass?

One full day included in your validity (not every day). You show QR to driver, get wristband. Buses every 20 minutes April-October, every 40 minutes in winter. I used it as free transport to Charlottenburg, not as loop tour.

Is Sanssouci Palace in Potsdam really included?

Yes, entry to Sanssouci Palace is included in all versions, plus ABC transport covers the S-Bahn to Potsdam. You still need a timed slot – reserve free on SPSG website after activating pass, or risk queuing 30-45 minutes at gate.

What’s the best time to visit Museum Island without crowds?

Tuesday to Thursday, 9:00-10:30am opening slot. I went Tuesday 9:15am and museums were 80% empty until 11am. By 11:30, DDR Museum line snakes outside. Monday many museums closed – avoid Monday with this pass.

Can I cancel or share Berlin City Pass?

Non-transferable after first activation, but refundable before activation if you buy direct from TurboPass. First scan starts the clock – 48h means exactly 48 hours, not calendar days. Activate at 10am Tuesday, expires 10am Thursday.

Is Berlin pass better than paying individually for Museum Island?

Museum Island alone costs €22 for all museums day ticket. If you only do Museum Island and nothing else, single ticket is cheaper. The City Pass wins when you add TV Tower, boat, DDR Museum and transport – my 3-day saved €98.50 vs individual.
