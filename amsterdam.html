Amsterdam City Pass Review: I Actually Tested It For 48 Hours (And Made Mistakes So You Don't Have To)

I bought the Amsterdam City Pass on a drizzly Thursday in March 2025, standing outside Amsterdam Centraal with a coffee that cost €4.80 and a suitcase wheel that squeaked. I had 48 hours, a list of museums I wanted to see, and that classic tourist fear: spending more time in ticket lines than actually looking at art. Here's what actually happened.

Spoiler: I saved money, but only because I planned like a maniac. If you just wing it, this Amsterdam city pass can flop. Let me break down the real deal.

Why I Ditched The I Amsterdam Card And Picked Turbopass

Everyone on Reddit screams "get the I amsterdam City Card" - it has public transport. But I was staying in Jordaan, walking everywhere. My feet hate trams. The Turbopass Amsterdam City Pass (the one sold as Amsterdam City Pass) is different: it's attraction-heavy, with 30+ things including Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh Museum (reserved slot, huge), Heineken Experience, A'DAM Lookout, and a canal cruise plus airport transfer. No GVB, but honestly? You don't need it if you're central.

My 2-day pass in late March 2025 was €129.00 for adult when I bought it direct. I checked again yesterday: 1-day is sitting around €79, 2-day €129, 3-day €149, and 5-day €199. Kids are way cheaper - that's how I compared with Go City which was pricier per day in their all-inclusive. Prices shift slightly in high summer (June-August adds about €10), so lock it before you fly.

Activation was simple: I got a QR code instantly, no app download hell. You show it on your phone. First scan = timer starts. My timer started Friday 09:12 at Rijksmuseum.

What My QR Code Actually Unlocked (And What I Skipped On Purpose)

The list looks massive, but I was realistic. I did NOT attempt Muiderslot Castle (it's 40 minutes out, ferry at 10:45 only, I learned that the hard way in 2024) or the stadium tours. I wanted Dutch masters, beer, a high viewpoint, and a boat.

This is what I actually used in 2 days:

  • Rijksmuseum – Normally €25 at the door now. I walked in at 9:15am, pre-booked slot for 9:30 via pass link. Queue for non-pass: 35 minutes in the rain. My queue: 4 minutes because they have a separate pre-booked line at the left side entrance. Don't go to the main middle entrance.
  • Van Gogh Museum – This is the reason to buy the pass. Individual tickets are €33 and sell out 3 weeks ahead. With the pass, you still need to reserve a timeslot in their portal (read the tiny email) but it WAS available even when public site said sold out. I went at 11:30am Saturday, walked straight in.
  • Heineken Experience – €26.50 walk-up. I went Friday 16:00, thought it would be quiet. It wasn't. 22-minute line even with pass. But they give you two beers at the end on the rooftop, and the view of De Pijp is underrated.
  • A'DAM Lookout + Swing – €28 for Lookout + €10 extra for the swing if you buy separately. Included. I went at 18:45 for sunset. No queue. Locals go for drinks at MADAM bar upstairs and don't realize the pass includes the deck. Best photos of Centraal side.
  • 100 Highlights Canal Cruise (Stromma) – €21 value. Departures from Damrak, every 20 minutes. I took the 10:00 one before crowds. You get earphones but skip them, sit on left side (port) for Anne Frank House view.
  • BONUS: Airport transfer train – Included return Amsterdam Schiphol to Centraal (€6.30 each way). Saved me fumbling with OV-chip.

Things I skipped that the pass offers: Moco Museum (€22.95), Rembrandt House (€21.05), THIS IS HOLLAND 5D (€26.50), Diamond Museum. I skipped because I was museumed-out.

My Exact 48-Hour Route In Amsterdam (Steal This)

Day 1 - Museumplein Heavy: Rijksmuseum 09:30-12:00. I walked to Van Gogh? No, I actually saved Van Gogh for day 2 because back-to-back big museums fries your brain. So I did coffee at CT Coffee & Coconuts (5 min walk, locals, not the tourist trap next to museum). Then 13:00 Rembrandt House? No – I pivoted to THIS IS HOLLAND at 14:30 because it was near the Lookout ferry. Free ferry F3 behind Centraal (3 mins) to Overhoofd, then Lookout at 18:45 sunset. Heineken at 16:00 slotted in because it's near De Pijp market – grab fries at Manneken Pis afterwards.

Day 2 - Centrum + Water: Van Gogh 11:30 (slept in, needed it), then walked through Nine Streets to Stromma canal cruise at 14:00. Used the airport train later at 17:20 back to Schiphol for checkout.

Crucial tip: the canal cruise line is shortest at 10:00-11:00. After 13:00 at Damrak, it's 45+ minutes in peak. Guides told me that themselves.

3 Insider Hacks They Don't Put On The Sales Page

1. The Left Door at Rijksmuseum

Everyone queues at the big glass entrance on Museumstraat. If you have a pre-booked slot (which Turbopass gives you via their booking portal), use the small priority entrance on the left side facing the gardens. I timed it: 4 minutes vs 38 minutes for ticket-holders without reservations at 09:30 Friday.

2. Sunset Swing Trick at A'DAM

The Over The Edge swing closes 30 mins before Lookout does. Go 90 minutes before sunset, ride the swing with light, then stay for sunset drinks. You avoid the Instagram crowd that arrives exactly at sunset and queues 25 minutes for the swing. The bartender at MADAM told me best light is 18:00-19:30 in March.

3. Jordaan Cafe Hideout Near Anne Frank (Where You Actually Get A Seat)

Between cruise and museums, you will be tired. Skip the cafes on Prinsengracht directly opposite Anne Frank – queue city. Walk 2 minutes to Cafe de Tuin on Tweede Tuindwarsstraat. Local brown cafe, no tourists, €3.50 coffee, apple pie bigger than your head. I wrote half this review there. It's where Jordaaners actually go.

Who Should NOT Buy This Pass?

If you're here for coffee shops, Vondelpark picnics, and just strolling red lights, don't bother. If you're staying 5 days and want to do one thing per day super slow, the I amsterdam Card with transport might suit more. Also if you're under 18, Van Gogh is €17 and Rijksmuseum is €12.50 – your break-even is harder, stick to the youth pass price which is €45 for Turbopass.

And a warning: Van Gogh Museum slot with Turbopass still requires manual reservation within 48h of activation. I almost missed it because I didn't read the second email. Do it immediately after buying.

Frequently Asked Questions About Amsterdam City Pass

Is the Amsterdam City Pass worth it for 2 days?

Yes if you visit Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh Museum and two extras like Heineken Experience and A'DAM Lookout. My real spend was €146.10 individually versus €129 for the pass, saving about €17 and skipping sold-out risk for Van Gogh.

Does Amsterdam City Pass include Van Gogh Museum?

Yes, Turbopass includes Van Gogh Museum with a reserved timeslot. You must book your slot via the link in your confirmation email within the pass validity. Public tickets sell out weeks ahead, but pass slots are often still available.

How much does Amsterdam City Pass cost in 2025?

In 2025 Turbopass pricing is around €79 for 1 day, €129 for 2 days, €149 for 3 days and €199 for 5 days for adults. Youth 14-17 is about €45 for 2 days, kids 4-13 cheaper. Summer adds roughly €10.

Does Amsterdam City Pass include public transport?

No, Turbopass Amsterdam City Pass does not include GVB tram metro bus. It includes a Schiphol airport train return and canal cruise. If you need unlimited transit, consider I amsterdam City Card instead.

How do I use the Amsterdam City Pass at Rijksmuseum?

After purchase, reserve your Rijksmuseum timeslot via Turbopass portal. On arrival, use the priority pre-booked entrance on the left side, not the main queue. My wait was 4 minutes versus 35 minutes for walk-up tickets.

Can I visit Anne Frank House with Amsterdam City Pass?

No, Anne Frank House is not included in any Amsterdam city pass. Tickets are sold only via annefrank.org exactly six weeks ahead at 10:00 AM CET and sell out in minutes. Book separately.

Does the pass include a canal cruise?

Yes, a 100 Highlights canal cruise with Stromma is included. Board at Damrak. Best times are before 11:00 AM to avoid 40+ minute lines. Sit on the left side for the best views of Anne Frank House and Westerkerk.

What is the difference between Amsterdam City Pass and I amsterdam Card?

Turbopass City Pass focuses on top attractions like Van Gogh, Rijksmuseum, Heineken and Lookout plus airport train. I amsterdam Card includes 70+ museums and unlimited GVB transport but is more expensive and does not always include Van Gogh.

Do I need to book attractions in advance with the pass?

Yes for Van Gogh Museum and Rijksmuseum you must reserve a slot via Turbopass booking links. For Heineken, A'DAM Lookout and canal cruise you can walk up, but reservations help during weekends and high season.

How long is Amsterdam City Pass valid after purchase?

The pass is valid for one year after purchase and only activates on first scan at an attraction. If your trip changes, you can use it later within that year. After activation, it runs for consecutive calendar days.
