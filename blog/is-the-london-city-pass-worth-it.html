Quick answer

Yes, the London City Pass is worth it if you will visit at least three to four paid headline attractions in the pass window. The Turbopass London City Pass bundles entry to around 20 attractions, including the Tower of London, Madame Tussauds, the London Dungeon, SEA LIFE and London Zoo, plus a 24-hour hop-on hop-off bus and a Thames river cruise, for 1 to 5 days. At the time of writing, resellers list it from roughly £110 for one day. If you mainly plan to see London's free museums, or only one or two paid sights, buy individual tickets instead. London is one of the most expensive cities in the world to sightsee. A single adult ticket to some of the biggest attractions can cost well over £30, and buying three or four of them on the day adds up fast, especially for families. That is the problem a city pass is meant to solve: one upfront price that covers a bundle of attractions, plus transport-style extras like a hop-on hop-off bus and a river cruise.

But a pass only saves money if you actually use it. This guide breaks down exactly what the Turbopass London City Pass includes, how to work out whether it pays off for your trip, which itineraries get the most value from 1, 2, 3 and 5-day passes, and which travelers are better off skipping it. We also show you how to combine it with London's excellent free museums so you see more without spending more.

Key takeaways

  • The pass suits first-time visitors who want to see several big-ticket sights in a short trip.
  • Break-even usually comes at three to four paid attractions, depending on which ones and how many days you buy.
  • Pair it with free museums (British Museum, National Gallery, Natural History Museum, Tate Modern) to build full days without extra cost.
  • Use the hop-on hop-off bus and river cruise days as your "transport and rest" days.
  • Check opening hours, booking rules and holiday closures before each pass day; winter hours are shorter.

What is the London City Pass from Turbopass?

The London City Pass is a digital sightseeing pass sold by Turbopass. You buy it online, receive it by email and in an app, then show it (along with photo ID where required) at each attraction. Passes are typically available for 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 consecutive days, and the pass can usually be activated at any point within a year of purchase, which makes it easy to buy early when you see a promotion.

What attractions are included?

The exact list changes from time to time, so always check the current list on the Turbopass page before you buy. Listings for the pass have included:

  • Landmarks and palaces: Tower of London, Kensington Palace and Hampton Court Palace.
  • Big entertainment attractions: Madame Tussauds London, The London Dungeon, SEA LIFE London Aquarium, Shrek's Adventure! London and the climb over the roof of The O2 (Up at The O2).
  • Family favorites: ZSL London Zoo.
  • Getting around: a 24-hour hop-on hop-off bus ticket and a 24-hour Thames river cruise.
  • Guided tours: walking tours such as a Harry Potter–themed London tour, a National Gallery guided tour and a city walk timed with the Changing of the Guard.
  • Optional add-on: the London Eye, which you can add when you buy.

That mix is the pass's main strength. Instead of a list of 100 small museums you will never visit, it focuses on a smaller set of high-value attractions that most first-time visitors already want to see.

How much does the London City Pass cost?

Turbopass sets prices by the number of days and by adult or child. At the time of writing, reseller listings start at around £110 for a 1-day adult pass, with multi-day passes costing more in total but less per day. Turbopass and its partners run regular promotions, so the price on the day you buy may be lower. Always confirm the live price on the official page.

How to work out whether it saves you money

You do not need a spreadsheet; you need five minutes and a list.

  1. Write down every paid attraction you genuinely want to see. Be honest. If you would not pay full price for it, do not count it.
  2. Look up the standard online adult price for each one on its official website.
  3. Add the value of extras you would actually use, such as a hop-on hop-off bus tour or a Thames cruise.
  4. Compare the total with the pass price for the number of days you need.
  5. Check you can fit them in. Count travel time, queues and meals. Three big attractions a day is a lot; two plus a cruise is realistic.

If your total is clearly higher than the pass price and you can fit everything in comfortably, the pass is worth it.

Who should buy the London City Pass?

Great fit

  • First-time visitors who want to see the Tower of London and two or three big entertainment attractions.
  • Families with children who will do the Zoo, SEA LIFE, Shrek's Adventure and the Dungeon (for older kids).
  • Short city breaks of 2–3 days where you want a structured plan and less time comparing ticket prices.
  • Travelers who like the hop-on hop-off bus as a way to get oriented on day one.

Probably skip it

  • Museum lovers who mainly plan to visit the British Museum, the National Gallery, the Natural History Museum or Tate Modern. General entry to these is free.
  • Slow travelers who prefer one attraction a day and lots of wandering.
  • Repeat visitors who have already seen the Tower and the big entertainment venues.
  • Visitors focused on theatre, shopping or food, where a sightseeing pass adds little.

Sample itineraries to get the most value

These plans assume a central London base and a mix of the pass and free sights. Check opening times on the day, especially in winter when many attractions close earlier.

1-day pass: the highlights

  • Morning: Tower of London as soon as it opens, to see the Crown Jewels before the queues build.
  • Midday: Walk over Tower Bridge, then board the Thames river cruise west toward Westminster.
  • Afternoon: Madame Tussauds or SEA LIFE, depending on your group.
  • Evening: Free: walk along the South Bank and see Big Ben and the Houses of Parliament lit up.

2-day pass: history plus fun

  • Day 1: Hop-on hop-off bus loop for orientation, Changing of the Guard walk, Kensington Palace.
  • Day 2: Tower of London, river cruise, London Dungeon in the afternoon.

3-day pass: the classic first trip

  • Day 1: Tower of London, Tower Bridge, river cruise to Westminster.
  • Day 2: Bus tour, Madame Tussauds, a free afternoon at the British Museum.
  • Day 3: London Zoo in Regent's Park in the morning, Up at The O2 in the afternoon for skyline views.

5-day pass: slow but complete

Add Hampton Court Palace as a half-day trip by train, a Harry Potter walking tour, the National Gallery guided tour, and free days at the Natural History Museum and Tate Modern. Five days lets you do two pass attractions a day without rushing.

Free London attractions to pair with your pass

London has some of the best free museums in the world. Mixing them with pass days stretches your budget and gives you rest days for tired legs.

  • British Museum in Bloomsbury.
  • National Gallery on Trafalgar Square.
  • Natural History Museum, Science Museum and V&A in South Kensington, all close together.
  • Tate Modern on the South Bank, with free views from its upper level.
  • Sky Garden in the City, free with advance booking.
  • Royal Parks such as Hyde Park, St James's Park and Regent's Park.

VisitLondon keeps an up-to-date list of free attractions, and special exhibitions inside free museums may still charge.

How to get around London with a pass

The London City Pass is a sightseeing pass, not a public transport card. For the Tube, buses and most trains in central London, use contactless pay-as-you-go with a bank card or phone, or an Oyster card. Transport for London applies daily and weekly fare caps, so you will not pay more than the cap however many trips you make. The pass's hop-on hop-off bus and river cruise are tourist services and work alongside, not instead of, public transport.

Tips for using the London City Pass

  1. Plan your pass days in advance. Some attractions require you to reserve a time slot, even with a pass. Book these as soon as you know your dates.
  2. Start early. Headline sights are quietest at opening time.
  3. Cluster by area. Tower of London, Tower Bridge and the river cruise pier are close together; Kensington Palace pairs well with the South Kensington museums.
  4. Check winter hours and holiday closures. Many attractions shorten their hours in winter and close on Christmas Day and Boxing Day.
  5. Carry photo ID. Some venues check ID against the pass name.
  6. Keep your phone charged. The pass lives in an app and email; a power bank is worth packing.
  7. Use the bus and cruise as breaks. They double as transport and a chance to sit down.

London City Pass vs other London passes

There are several London sightseeing passes on the market. They differ in which attractions they include, how many days they cover and whether you can choose a fixed number of attractions or unlimited entry within the day count. The Turbopass London City Pass focuses on a shorter list of high-value headline attractions plus transport-style extras. Before you choose any pass, compare three things: whether your must-see attractions are included, how many you can realistically do per day, and the total price for your number of days.

Winter and Christmas visits

Visiting London in December? Christmas lights on Regent Street and Oxford Street, winter markets and festive ice rinks are free or low-cost highlights. Days are short, so plan indoor pass attractions such as Madame Tussauds, SEA LIFE and the Dungeon for late afternoons, and keep outdoor sights like the Tower of London and the Zoo for mornings. Check closures over 24–26 December, when many attractions and some transport services close or run reduced hours.

What to expect at the Tower of London

The Tower of London is often the single most valuable entry on the pass, so it is worth planning properly.

  • Arrive at opening time. The Crown Jewels queue grows quickly from mid-morning, especially at weekends and in school holidays.
  • See the Crown Jewels first, then the White Tower, the medieval palace and the walls.
  • Join a Yeoman Warder tour if one is running; they are entertaining and full of stories.
  • Allow three to four hours. Rushing the Tower in 90 minutes to squeeze in another attraction usually means you see less.
  • Pair it with the river. Tower Pier is right next door, which makes the Tower and the Thames cruise a natural combination.

A full family day with the pass

Families get some of the best value from the London City Pass, because children's tickets add up quickly and the pass covers several kid-friendly attractions close together.

  • Morning: ZSL London Zoo in Regent's Park. Go early when animals are most active and before the busiest crowds.
  • Lunch: A picnic in Regent's Park, or a café near the zoo.
  • Afternoon: Head to the South Bank for SEA LIFE London Aquarium and Shrek's Adventure!, which sit close together near Westminster Bridge.
  • Evening: Walk along the river to see the Houses of Parliament and Big Ben lit up, then take the Tube back.

Check the age guidance before visiting the London Dungeon; its actors and dark rooms are designed to scare, and younger children may find it too intense.

Where to stay to make the most of a pass

Staying central saves travel time, which matters when you are paying for a set number of pass days.

  • South Bank and Waterloo: Walkable to SEA LIFE, the London Eye, the river cruise piers and Westminster.
  • The City and Tower Hill: Close to the Tower of London and Tower Bridge, quieter at weekends.
  • Kensington and South Kensington: Near Kensington Palace and the free museums; great for families.
  • Covent Garden and the West End: Central for theatre, restaurants and Madame Tussauds via the Tube.

Wherever you stay, check that a Tube or Elizabeth line station is within a short walk; it makes early starts far easier.

Is the London Eye add-on worth it?

The London Eye is one of the most recognisable rides in the city, and the optional add-on lets you buy it with the pass instead of separately. It is worth it if a view over the Thames is high on your list and you would buy a ticket anyway. It is less important if you already plan to visit Up at The O2 or the free Sky Garden for skyline views. If you add it, aim for a clear late afternoon so you see the city in daylight and as the lights come on, and expect security checks and queues at busy times.

How to activate and use your digital pass

After you buy, Turbopass sends your pass by email with access to its app. Download the app before you travel, log in, and save your pass for offline use in case your phone signal is weak inside attractions. Read the instructions for each attraction in the app: some scan the pass directly at the entrance, some ask you to collect a ticket first, and some need a reservation. Your pass days count from first use, so do not scan it at a small attraction late on your arrival day unless you plan to use that day fully.

Frequently asked questions

Is the London City Pass worth it for one day?

It can be, if you plan three paid attractions in that day, such as the Tower of London, a river cruise and Madame Tussauds. It is a busy day, so start early. If you only want one or two attractions, buy separate tickets.

What is included in the London City Pass?

Listings have included around 20 attractions and experiences, such as the Tower of London, Kensington Palace, Madame Tussauds, the London Dungeon, SEA LIFE, London Zoo, Up at The O2, a 24-hour hop-on hop-off bus, a 24-hour Thames cruise and guided walking tours, with the London Eye as an optional add-on. Check the current list before you buy.

Does the London City Pass include public transport?

No. It includes a tourist hop-on hop-off bus and a river cruise, but not the Tube or city buses. Use contactless or Oyster for public transport.

How long is the London City Pass valid?

You choose 1 to 5 days of use. Listings say the pass can be activated within 12 months of purchase, and the day count runs from first use.

Do I need to book attractions in advance with the pass?

Some attractions require a timed reservation even with a pass. Check the instructions in the Turbopass app and book those slots as soon as your dates are set.

Is the London City Pass good for kids?

Yes, especially for families who want the Zoo, SEA LIFE, Shrek's Adventure and the bus tour. Child passes are cheaper. The London Dungeon has age guidance and can be scary for younger children.

Which free museums should I visit with the pass?

The British Museum, National Gallery, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, V&A and Tate Modern all offer free general entry and fit easily around pass days.

The bottom line

The London City Pass is worth it for first-time visitors who want to see three or four paid headline attractions within a few days, and it is especially good value for families and short city breaks. Make your list, compare prices, plan your days by area, and fill the gaps with London's free museums. If your plan is mostly free sights, skip the pass and buy individual tickets.
